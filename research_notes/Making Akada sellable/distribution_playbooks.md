# Distribution playbooks for campus-first student apps, and precedent for "shared course data" network effects (as of Sept 2026)

## Key question 1: Campus-by-campus launch playbooks (Facebook, Tinder, Bumble, Snapchat, Fizz, Sidechat/Yik Yak, Saturn, GroupMe, Notion Campus Leaders, Quizlet, Studocu / Course Hero, Coursicle, RateMyProfessors, Discord)

### Takeaway
Every successful campus-first app used the same skeleton: pick one dense campus, seed it through a small number of socially central students (paid trivially or not at all), make the product useless-until-your-friends-join so that adoption inside a school snowballs, and only then copy the recipe school-by-school. The ones that were student-built and free (Fizz, Saturn, Sidechat, Quizlet, Coursicle) reached 70–95% penetration of a school in weeks-to-months on ambassador stipends in the low hundreds of dollars; the things that stalled them were trust/safety failures (fake accounts, bogus data, harassment), bugs at the back-to-school spike, and (for Coursicle) trying to sell to the university instead of to students.

### Cited Findings

**Saturn (schedule/social calendar; high school first, then college)**
- Co-founded by Dylan Diamond and Max Baron; Diamond built the first version in 2015 as a high-school junior; the two met as sophomores at Penn in 2019; the company has raised about $62M — [EdTech Digest, Jul 2024](https://www.edtechdigest.com/2024/07/11/young-visionaries-seek-to-transform-student-life-with-saturn-app/); [Built In NYC](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/saturn-raises-44m-hiring-social-calendar-app)
- "Initial word-of-mouth growth led to penetration rates rising from 30% to as high as 80–90% in certain high schools"; Saturn "now serves millions of users across 18,000 schools nationwide"; during back-to-school it has hit #2 overall in the App Store and #1 in Productivity — [Sourcery.vc, "The Viral Gen Z App backed by Jeff Bezos..."](https://www.sourcery.vc/p/the-viral-gen-z-app-backed-by-jeff); same story framed as a side hustle that raised $40M+ — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/starting-a-business/gen-z-focused-side-hustle-leads-to-40m-investment/474270)
- Saturn is the subject of a Lenny's Newsletter case study on building a viral consumer app (blocked in-session; the report writer should treat it as the best single deep-dive on Saturn's mechanics) — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/lessons-on-building-a-viral-consumer)
- School-level launches are run as student "chapters": at Richard Montgomery HS the student who launched the chapter "was originally encouraged by Saturn's Growth Outreach Coordinator to launch the chapter" — i.e. a company employee recruits one student per school to start it — [The Tide (RM student paper)](https://thermtide.com/13076/news/saturn-app-launches-at-rm/)
- Profiles let students link their TikTok, Snapchat and Instagram, and the app shows classmates' schedules — [Tracxn profile](https://tracxn.com/d/companies/saturn/__zuPMDUyFrH5Snnm3f5PhGx-5kbrM-AdcwqtaDxm05oA)
- What stalled it (Aug 2023): the app hit the top of the App Store charts in August 2023 and simultaneously triggered a national student-data-privacy scare; security researchers showed the verification flow let non-students join a school's community, and "the app doesn't specify whether a teacher teaches that subject, so you could build a schedule that has completely bogus classes"; anyone could log in with a Snapchat account, create a bogus schedule at a school and then see and message real students — [WMBF News, Aug 16 2023](https://www.wmbfnews.com/2023/08/16/grand-strand-cyber-security-experts-raise-concerns-new-student-scheduling-app); [ClickOrlando, Aug 17 2023](https://clickorlando.com/news/local/2023/08/17/what-is-the-saturn-app-why-student-scheduler-has-parents-schools-worried); [Defend Young Minds](https://www.defendyoungminds.com/post/is-saturn-app-safe-since-security-update-we-posed-as-a-student-to-unmask-dangers)
- Fix: Saturn (blog post attributed in coverage to Chris Hladczuk) moved to requiring a school-issued email or verified phone number for full access to a school's network — [WMBF News](https://www.wmbfnews.com/2023/08/16/grand-strand-cyber-security-experts-raise-concerns-new-student-scheduling-app)
- Bugs at peak season were a second drag: a student op-ed in Oct 2023 was titled "Saturn's app functionality orbits out of control with constant bugs" — [Inklings News, Oct 6 2023](https://inklingsnews.com/opinions/2023/10/06/saturns-app-functionality-orbits-out-of-control-with-constant-bugs)

### Inferences
- The unit of launch is one school, not "students." Fizz's numbers (95% of ~7,600 at Stanford; 70% at Rice) and Saturn's (30% → 80–90% per high school) show that a free, student-built app can saturate a single institution; LUMS (~5,000) is the same order of magnitude as Stanford undergrad, so "80%+ of LUMS" is a realistic ceiling to aim at, not a fantasy.
- Ambassador economics are trivially cheap: one Fizz lead ambassador at Pomona cost $265 total; Saturn uses one volunteer "chapter" founder per school prompted by a single growth staffer; Sidechat used cash-per-download and paid student orgs. For a zero-budget founder, the equivalent is one respected student per batch/major, paid in status and early features rather than cash.
- The campus newspaper is a recurring free channel: Coursicle collected a "Coursicle comes to X" article at nearly every school; Saturn and Sidechat launches were covered by student papers within weeks. Pitching the LUMS student publications and society newsletters is the analogue.
- The things that killed momentum were all trust failures at the moment of peak attention: Saturn's fake-schedule/verification scandal landed the same week it hit #2 in the App Store; Fizz's problems were harassment/moderation; Yik Yak users revolted at a forced redesign. For Akada the equivalent risk is a wrong deadline or a stranger seeing a student's plan; verification (LUMS email) and edit provenance should exist before the first big push, not after.
- Coursicle's detour is the most instructive negative: selling to the university (thousands of emails, two replies) stalled it for years; going student-direct with a single sharp hook (a text when a seat opens) plus a competitor's shutdown is what grew it. The LUMS registrar is not the customer.

## Key question 2: Shared course data as a network effect (Saturn, Coursicle, Studocu, Quizlet, Piazza, syllabus tools) — did it produce growth, and what about data quality, instructor pushback and syllabus copyright?

### Takeaway
There is solid precedent that data one student enters and classmates reuse produces growth (Quizlet's class sets, Saturn's per-school schedules, Studocu/Course Hero's upload-to-unlock libraries, Piazza's class rooms), and equally solid precedent that the failure mode is trust: unverified contributors created "completely bogus classes" on Saturn, and note-sharing sites drew organised instructor pushback. The facts in a syllabus (dates, weights, readings) are not what copyright protects, and Texas law even mandates public posting of syllabi with "major assignments and examinations" — but the syllabus document itself is the instructor's expression, so the safe design stores extracted deadlines/weights, not the PDF.

### Cited Findings
- Saturn's whole product is crowdsourced schedule data per school: it "integrates directly with high school class schedules to allow students to coordinate," and its growth came from word of mouth to 80–90% of a school — [ClickOrlando](https://clickorlando.com/news/local/2023/08/17/what-is-the-saturn-app-why-student-scheduler-has-parents-schools-worried); [Sourcery.vc](https://www.sourcery.vc/p/the-viral-gen-z-app-backed-by-jeff)
- Data-quality/trust failure on Saturn: "the app doesn't specify whether a teacher teaches that subject, so you could build a schedule that has completely bogus classes"; non-students could join a school; fixed by requiring school email or verified phone — [WMBF News](https://www.wmbfnews.com/2023/08/16/grand-strand-cyber-security-experts-raise-concerns-new-student-scheduling-app); [Defend Young Minds](https://www.defendyoungminds.com/post/is-saturn-app-safe-since-security-update-we-posed-as-a-student-to-unmask-dangers)
- Quizlet's growth was literally classmates reusing one student's sets: built for one French exam, spread "amongst friends and fellow students," no advertising, half of US high-schoolers by 2017 — [Able Magazine](https://ablemagazine.co.uk/interactive-learning-platform-quizlet-seeks-expansion-in-the-uk/); [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/quizlet-s-growth-puts-it-on-the-top-of-the-edtech-stack)
- Studocu/Course Hero: contribution gates ("upload to unlock", 5 unlocks per 10 uploads) built a library "at zero production cost" whose pages rank in search and pull in new users; Studocu's investors call the network effects "compelling" and the business "exceptionally capital efficient" — [Partech](https://partechpartners.com/news/partech-growth-invests-in-studocu); [Wikipedia: Course Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_Hero); [IdeaUsher](https://ideausher.com/blog/launch-platform-studocu/)
- Piazza's unit of adoption is the class: one instructor creates the room and the whole enrolment joins; 100,000 students and professors in year one, later 1M students across 1,500 universities — [Vator](https://vator.tv/2012-01-06-piazza-raises-6m-for-social-learning-network-0/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220119005317/en/Piazza-Appoints-EdTech-Leader-Ethan-ORafferty-as-its-New-CEO)
- Coursicle's course database is scraped/licensed from "over 900 colleges", not student-entered — the growth loop there was the seat-alert hook plus word of mouth, not shared data — [High Ground News](https://www.highgroundnews.com/innovationnews/Coursicle.aspx); [Orbit Marketing](https://orbitmarketing.substack.com/p/joe-puccio-founder-of-coursicle-saving-314)
- Instructor pushback exists and is organised: sites "encourage students to post documents to gain access to other documents, including syllabi, notes, and exam keys" (Quizlet, StudyBlue, OneClass, Course Hero named); an instructor's "first and most important tool is the syllabus," and faculty are told to add language prohibiting sharing of course materials to Chegg/Course Hero — [UMass Amherst copyright office](https://websites.umass.edu/copyright/2015/10/23/student-note-sharing-services); [Higher Ed Praxis](https://higheredpraxis.substack.com/p/tip-protecting-course-content-from)
- Copyright line: "Instructors hold copyright on their original work, including slides, lecture notes, assignments... However, instructors don't hold copyright in the ideas embedded in their lectures or syllabi"; students hold copyright in their own notes and may share those — [UC Davis](https://vlcross.ucdavis.edu/blog/students-sharing-course-material); [University of Alberta](https://www.ualberta.ca/copyright/about-the-copyright-office/news/2020/sharing-your-teaching-materials-online.html); [Wesleyan College LibGuide](https://wesleyancollege.libguides.com/copyright/coursesharing); [Toronto Metropolitan University](https://www.torontomu.ca/content/dam/graduate/student-guide/academic-matters/procedures/aio-resources/3_CopyrightforStudents.pdf)
- Public-record precedent: Texas HB 2504 (2009) requires public universities to post, for every undergraduate course, "a course syllabus that includes major course requirements, major assignments and examinations, learning objectives... and required or recommended readings," within seven days of term start, reachable in three clicks from the homepage with no login — [UT Austin Provost](https://provost.utexas.edu/policies-and-compliance/hb2504); [Lamar State College](https://www.lamarpa.edu/General/Course-and-Faculty-Information-(HB-2504)); [Texas Public Policy Foundation](https://www.texaspolicy.com/texas-becoming-a-model-for-higher-education-transparency/)
- A university library at U of T runs a "Submit a syllabus" collection, i.e. institutions themselves crowdsource syllabi — [UTM Library](https://utm.library.utoronto.ca/node/49)

### Inferences
- Akada's proposed loop ("your section's deadlines are already loaded") is structurally Saturn's loop (one student enters the school schedule, everyone else just selects), applied at the section level. Saturn shows it works and shows the exact hole: unverified contributors plus no provenance = bogus data and a trust crisis. Minimum design: LUMS-email verification, per-field "entered by / confirmed by N classmates" badges, and edit history.
- The section-level network is tiny (a LUMS section is tens of students), so the loop is "first uploader seeds the section" rather than a campus-wide effect. That is a feature for a solo founder: one syllabus upload per section per semester (roughly hundreds of uploads for all of LUMS) is a target a single person can finish by hand in the first two weeks of a semester, which is exactly Facebook's course-list tactic (see KQ6).
- Copyright risk is low if Akada stores extracted facts (dates, weights, topics, reading titles) and does not republish the syllabus PDF to other students; the same institutions that police note-sharing sites concede that ideas and facts in a syllabus are not protected, and Texas treats the assignment/exam schedule as public information.
- Instructor pushback is directed at exam keys and lecture notes, not at deadline lists; no source found of an instructor objecting to students sharing due dates. A courtesy "instructors can claim/verify their course" feature would turn the most likely objector into a validator (Piazza's instructor-led adoption is the model).

- No source found of a study-planner/deadline app where a wrong crowdsourced deadline caused a documented user backlash; the Saturn "bogus classes" case is the closest analogue.

## Key question 6: "Good sales / customer experience / support" for a consumer student app — does high-touch, do-things-that-don't-scale onboarding work as a launch tactic?

### Takeaway
Yes, and it is the standard pattern: Facebook's founders hand-built each university's course list before launch (a direct precedent for "I'll load your syllabus"), Tinder's founders threw a party you could only enter after installing the app and tracked adoption house by house, Snapchat spread from one relative's high school, and Coursicle's founder eventually grew by giving students a single concrete benefit rather than selling to administrations. Paul Graham's essay formalises it: nearly every startup has to recruit its first users by hand.

### Cited Findings
- "The most common unscalable thing founders have to do at the start is to recruit users manually. Nearly all startups have to"; "startups take off because the founders make them take off" — [Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale"](https://paulgraham.com/ds.HTML); summaries — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/summary-do-things-that-don-t-scale-by-paul-graham-bdcb54215a); [Medium](https://medium.com/@edisipka/my-notes-on-do-things-that-dont-scale-the-reality-check-i-needed-e986bad14620)
- Facebook: "when Mark Zuckerberg discussed this at Y Combinator, he talked about how hard it was to create a course list initially for Harvard and later for other universities, but doing that made students feel they have an exclusive platform for communication and many of them signed up promptly"; the same round-up notes startups that "manually onboarded" supply at fairs and one that "deployed physical servers at each university to scale" — [Entrepreneurship.de, "Startups do these 9 things that don't scale"](https://entrepreneurship.de/en/magazin/startups-do-these-9-things-that-dont-scale)
- Tinder: party entry only for people who had downloaded the app; founders "manually tracked adoption patterns across houses" — [Alexander Jarvis](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/tinder-doing-things-that-dont-scale/); collection of such cases — [Jarvis "Doing things that don't scale" collection](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/resources/collections/doing-things-that-dont-scale-collection/)
- Saturn: a company growth coordinator personally recruits one student to launch each school chapter — [The Tide](https://thermtide.com/13076/news/saturn-app-launches-at-rm/)
- Fizz: paid student hires run flyers, Instagram and in-app posting for their own campus — [The Student Life](https://tsl.news/fizz-social-media/)
- Coursicle: the founder's early plan of institutional partnerships failed ("thousands of emails and only 2 universities responding"); growth came from students telling students — [Venturist by SVS](https://venturistbysvs.substack.com/p/tech-ub-coursicle); [Lanthorn](https://lanthorn.com/?p=52147)
- Piazza's adoption path runs through the instructor creating the class room, i.e. one relationship per course — [Vator](https://vator.tv/2012-01-06-piazza-raises-6m-for-social-learning-network-0/)

### Inferences
- "I'll load your syllabus for you" is the Facebook course-list tactic in 2026 clothing, and because Akada extracts the syllabus through a Claude connector, the founder can pre-load every section's outline for the semester before students arrive; the "sales" then becomes a single sentence in the section group ("your section is already in").
- The measurable version of high-touch: sit in the first lecture week in a common area with a laptop, load syllabi on the spot, and count sections covered rather than signups — that mirrors Tinder tracking adoption by house and gives a real denominator (sections at LUMS) to report against.
- Support is a growth tactic here only insofar as it protects trust in the data (fix a wrong deadline within minutes, visibly); the Saturn and Yik Yak cases show what happens when the product breaks at peak attention.

### Gaps
- No case retrieved of a study/planner app founder documenting in-person dorm onboarding sessions with numbers (conversion per session, retention of hand-onboarded users).
- Paul Graham's Stripe "Collison installation" passage could not be quoted directly (page blocked); the essay is the canonical source for "install it on their laptop right now."

## Key question 8: Which referral mechanic fits — "invite a classmate", "send your friend the guide", or "your section's deadlines are already loaded"?

### Takeaway
Referral surfaces are more common in Education apps than in any category but Social/Health (38% of tracked Education apps have one), but the campus cases that actually grew did not run classic invite-for-reward loops: they ran utility loops where the product got better for me when my classmates joined (Saturn schedules, Quizlet sets, Fizz/Sidechat feeds, GroupMe class chats). The evidence favours "your section's deadlines are already loaded" (a pull loop) over "invite a classmate" (a push loop).

### Cited Findings
- Across 809 tracked apps, 25.3% have a referral/invite-friends surface; prevalence is ~50% in Social Networking and Health & Fitness, 38% in Education, 13% in News; such screens rose from 10.6% of captured apps in 2023 to 22.1% in 2024, then 19.0% in 2025 — [Lazyweb research](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/how-many-apps-have-a-referral-program); [Lazyweb: are referral screens still growing?](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/are-referral-and-invite-friends-screens-still-growing)
- Generic referral-loop mechanics and claims that referred users start with higher engagement — [Viral Loops](https://viral-loops.com/blog/mobile-app-referral-program/); Loom's referral loops case study (member-only) — [UX Collective](https://uxdesign.cc/looms-referral-loops-a-case-study-ea185b9adefa)
- Utility loops in the cases above: Saturn's value is seeing classmates' schedules, which requires classmates to be on it (30% → 80–90% per school by word of mouth) — [Sourcery.vc](https://www.sourcery.vc/p/the-viral-gen-z-app-backed-by-jeff); Quizlet spread as sets were shared among friends and classmates, with no advertising — [EdSurge](https://edsurge.com/news/quizlet-s-growth-puts-it-on-the-top-of-the-edtech-stack); Studocu/Course Hero use contribution gates ("upload to unlock") rather than invites — [Wikipedia: Course Hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Course_Hero); [The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/document-sharing-platform-studocu-raises-50m/)
- Paid-invite loops were used by Sidechat ("cash incentives for downloads") alongside ambassadors — [34th Street](https://34st.com/article/2022/04/sidechat-app-yik-yak-college-anonymous-social-media)
- Class-wide chats propagate by students adding friends before they meet (WashU class of 2022 chat >1,000 members before term) — [Student Life](https://www.studlife.com/scene/2018/08/23/youre-the-pizza-guy-inside-the-class-of-2022s-groupme-boom)
- Piazza's loop is top-down: one instructor enrols a whole class at once — [Vator](https://vator.tv/2012-01-06-piazza-raises-6m-for-social-learning-network-0/)

### Inferences
- "Your section's deadlines are already loaded" is the only one of the three that delivers value before signup and is worth forwarding by a third party (a CR or a friend) without a reward; it also gives the founder a supply-side lever (pre-load outlines) that does not depend on users inviting anyone. "Invite a classmate" adds nothing unless the invitee's presence changes the inviter's experience — so pair it with a real shared state (e.g., "3 classmates confirmed this deadline", shared reading progress, a group study block).
- "Send your friend the guide" (content loop) is the StudyTok/Quizlet shape and works cross-campus, but is weak at LUMS scale; it matters later, when a generic syllabus-import mode makes the guide useful to any student.
- Contribution gating (Studocu/Course Hero) is a plausible mechanic for sections Akada has not pre-loaded: "be the first to upload your section's outline and it unlocks for everyone" — but the Saturn lesson says gate uploads behind LUMS-email verification and show provenance.

### Gaps
- No A/B evidence comparing invite-reward vs. pre-loaded-data loops for study tools; the category-level prevalence stats say referral screens are common, not that they work.
- Whether Saturn ran an explicit "invite classmates" reward and what its K-factor was — not retrievable in-session (Lenny's case study likely covers this).
