# Student productivity / study-planner app market: who pays, for what, and why planners die (as of 29 Sep 2026)

Method note for the report writer (not a section): this note rests on ~45 web searches and their result snippets. Every direct page fetch (TechCrunch, Wikipedia, SEC, RevenueCat, Adapty, Business of Apps, Apple App Store, BusinessWire, MyStudyLife help centre, Forest, AOL) was rejected by the sandbox's egress proxy, and the session's search budget then ran out, so no page was read in full text. Numbers labelled (est.) come from third-party revenue estimators (Latka, Prospeo, Bitscale, AppGoblin) that are unaudited and routinely off by 2-3x. Pre-2024 data is labelled with its year. Where a search summary attributed a number to a page I could not open, I say so.

## Q1. Which student-facing apps actually make money from students, at what price, with what free/paid split, and which paid feature converts?

### Takeaway
The money in "student apps" sits in homework-help/test-prep and flashcards (Chegg, Course Hero, Studocu, Quizlet) and, since 2024, in AI note-takers (Turbo AI); those charge $8-20/month and put answers, practice tests, or generated notes behind the paywall. Pure planners (MyStudyLife, Shovel, iStudiez, Structured) price at $3-10/month, disclose no revenue, and the largest free planner (MyStudyLife) had to add ads; meanwhile Notion gives students its paid tier for free, and Chegg, the biggest "students pay" business, lost 40% of its subscribers in a year to generative AI.

### Inferences
- Willingness to pay scales with proximity to the grade. Answers (Chegg $15-20/mo), generated practice tests/flashcards (Quizlet $7.99, Knowt $5.99-12.99) and lecture-to-notes (Turbo AI ~$20) command 2-5x the price of scheduling (iStudiez $2.99/mo, MSL $4.99/mo, Shovel $9.79/mo). Every paid feature that demonstrably converts is an output the student cannot produce quickly themselves; none of them is "organise my deadlines".
- The planner category's ceiling is visible in its leaders' behaviour: MyStudyLife (millions of users) added ads; iStudiez moved from $2.99 one-time to $26.99/yr; Shovel needs a 7-day trial, an LMS listing and a lifetime SKU to sell at $9.79. If a planner with millions of users could not live on subscriptions, a solo planner will not either without a differentiated paid unit.
- Rough paid-share arithmetic (unverified inputs): Quizlet ~1M paying / 50M MAU is ~2%; Turbo AI "eight-figure ARR" ($10-99M) over 5M users at ~$20/mo implies roughly 0.4-4% paying at any moment. Low-single-digit paid conversion is the norm even for category winners.
- Notion's free Education Plus and Todoist's free tier set the student's price anchor for "a place to write my tasks" at $0. A planner can only charge for what those cannot do out of the box (grade projection from a grading scheme, syllabus parsing, spaced recall tied to courses).
- Chegg's -40% subscribers and Course Hero's cuts show that "students will pay for X" is not durable when a general AI can do X; a planner's grade-projection and recall features are less exposed than answers, but any AI-import feature is trivially replicable by ChatGPT/Claude plus a calendar.

### Gaps
- No company discloses its free/paid split or paid conversion rate; Quizlet Plus subscriber counts, Shovel/Structured/iStudiez/Forest/MyStudyLife revenues are all undisclosed. A search summary attributed "~$250K revenue on ~150K installs" to Structured, but I could not find the page carrying that claim, so it is not cited above.
- Turbo AI's exact ARR, paid conversion and retention are not public beyond "eight-figure".
- Todoist Pro's student price, GoodNotes/Notability revenue, Flora's premium price, Class Timetable, Studious and Studocu's USD price were not captured before the search budget ran out (GoodNotes/Notability search returned only feature comparisons).
- Course Hero's current revenue is hidden inside Learneo's "$200M+ combined" figure.

## Q2. What do surveys and platform data say about students' willingness to pay, by category, and which categories convert worst?

### Takeaway
No survey found measures what share of university students pay for study apps by category; the best available proxies are (a) very high GenAI usage (80% of undergraduates worldwide), (b) subscription-platform data showing Education and Productivity are among the hardest categories to monetise, with freemium apps converting ~2.1% of installs by day 35 versus ~10.7% for hard paywalls, and (c) revenue concentration where the top 10% of apps take 95% of subscription revenue. In Pakistan the only monetised consumer pattern found is exam prep at roughly Rs 500-600/month.

### Cited Findings

#### Student usage and attitude surveys (2025-2026)
- Chegg Global Student Survey 2025: 80% of undergraduates worldwide have used GenAI to support their studies; accuracy is the top concern — [BusinessWire, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250128439546/en/Chegg-Global-Student-Survey-2025-80-of-Undergraduates-Worldwide-Have-Used-GenAI-to-Support-their-Studies-But-Accuracy-a-Top-Concern); [Silicon UK](https://www.silicon.co.uk/press-release/chegg-global-student-survey-2025-80-of-undergraduates-worldwide-have-used-genai-to-support-their-studies-but-accuracy-a-top-concern). No spending question was surfaced.
- Campus surveys: 62% of first-year students use GenAI "frequently" or "occasionally" for coursework and ~85% used it in assignments in the past year (figures from the Tulane and CSU Chico institutional surveys; which figure belongs to which survey was not resolvable from snippets) — [Tulane Survey Says, Spring 2026](https://oair.tulane.edu/node/2831); [CSU Chico Fall 2025 AI survey](https://www.csuchico.edu/ir/_assets/documents/fall-25-ai-survey)
- 55% of college students use GenAI for brainstorming; 18% question the value of college (Sept 2025 survey) — [Gigazine](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250902-college-student-ai/)
- Willingness-to-pay for GenAI study tools (121 undergraduates, one large US public university): perceived ease of use predicts usefulness, which predicts intention, which predicts willingness to pay — [Research Square preprint](https://www.researchsquare.com/article/rs-10210622). Small sample; a model of drivers, not a price point.
- Older, non-US evidence: Chinese students' willingness to pay for mobile learning is driven by "personal payment awareness, perceived value and interpersonal influence" — [Inderscience](https://www.inderscience.com/offer.php?id=113462) (pre-2024); for knowledge-payment apps, "recognition of expectation and social impact" mattered most — [DOAJ, 2019](https://doaj.org/article/91b2eca9d2344e6f8b2da64c7a7e2ac0) (2019). ### Inferences
- Ranking categories by evidence of paid conversion: (1) test prep / exam-specific content (Chegg still had 2.6M payers at $15-20/mo mid-2025 even in collapse; Maqsad is the only Pakistani B2C pattern found), (2) flashcards with AI generation and practice tests (Quizlet, Knowt), (3) AI notes from lectures (Turbo AI's eight-figure ARR), (4) note-taking apps on iPad (no numbers captured), (5) planners/organisers, where the leaders are ad-supported at $3-5/mo, and (6) generic to-do, priced at zero for students by Notion and Todoist. Planners are at the wrong end.
- RevenueCat's 2.1% freemium day-35 conversion is the realistic planning number for a free planner with an upsell; the 10.7% hard-paywall number only applies if the app is unusable without paying, which for a planner means losing the "just try it" install.
- In Lahore the local price anchor for a study product students pay for is ~Rs 500-600/month (Maqsad); a planner would need to sit at or below that, i.e. roughly $2/month, unless it sells in USD to overseas students.

### Gaps
- No survey giving the percentage of university students who pay for any study app, by category, in the US, UK or Pakistan. No data on the share of students paying for ChatGPT Plus or Claude.
- RevenueCat's Education-specific install-to-paid, LTV and renewal figures exist on the blocked breakout page and could not be read; only the cross-category headline numbers above are cited.
- No Pakistani willingness-to-pay study for apps was found; the Pakistani surveys found are about online classes during COVID.

## Q3. What is known about retention for planner/habit apps among students, especially the drop-off after the first weeks of a semester?

### Takeaway
Education apps have among the worst retention of any category: roughly 14-15% of installers are still active on day 1 and 2-3% on day 30 in Adjust-style benchmarks (OneSignal's different method shows ~8% at day 30). Nobody publishes a week-4-of-semester churn curve, but the category's download and revenue peaks in late August to September plus 2-8% day-30 retention mean, by arithmetic, that most of a September cohort has gone by mid-semester.

### Cited Findings
- Education app day-30 retention is ~2%, one of the lowest of all sectors — [Business of Apps: education app benchmarks](https://www.businessofapps.com/?p=98187)
- Education apps: day-1 retention near 14-15%, day-30 in the 2-3% range (2.1% in one report) — [Passion.io retention benchmarks, updated Oct 2025](https://cms.passion.io/blog/mobile-app-retention-benchmarks-for-creators-course-coaching-apps)
- Cross-category baseline (Adjust): ~26% day-1 globally, ~23% in North America, ~5-6% by day 30 — [Passion.io citing Adjust](https://webflow.passion.io/blog/mobile-app-vs-web-app-coaching-business-retention)
- OneSignal 2024 benchmarks: 28.29% day 1, 17.86% day 7, 7.88% day 30 across all apps; Education 8.02% at day 30 — [Lovable guide citing OneSignal](https://lovable.dev/guides/what-is-a-good-retention-rate-for-an-app). The 2% vs 8% spread is a methodology difference (push-enabled cohorts vs all installs), not a real disagreement about the category.
- On why benchmarks vary and how to read them — [Phiture](https://phiture.com/mobilegrowthstack/managing-retention-rate-benchmarks-and-expectations/)
- Subscription retention is price-sensitive: lower-priced apps show 36% median retention vs 23% for high-priced apps; more than 60% of conversions happen inside the first week and cancellations of 3-day trials cluster on day 0-1 — [RevenueCat 2026 via Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news)
- Seasonality: education apps have had progressively stronger back-to-school seasons, 2023 the strongest to that date; September is ClassDojo's biggest revenue month — [Appfigures, Sept 2023](https://analytics.appfigures.com/resources/insights/20230908?f=5)
- Sensor Tower Q3 2024 (US, iOS): Duolingo weekly downloads peaked in late August at ~382K and weekly revenue climbed to ~$4.1M by mid-September; Remind's weekly downloads peaked at ~285K in early August — [Sensor Tower US Q3 2024](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-ios-top-5-education-units-us-600a1c7d241bc16eb8da0a79). Spain: Google Classroom spiked to ~60K weekly downloads in early September; Moodle to 25K mid-September — [Sensor Tower ES Q3 2024](https://sensortower.com/blog/2024-q3-ios-top-5-education-units-es-600a1c7d241bc16eb8da0a79)

### Inferences
- Combining the two datasets: acquisition for a student planner is concentrated in a 2-3 week window at term start, and 92-98% of those installers are inactive by day 30. That is the "week 4 cliff" the founder asked about; it is inferred from category benchmarks, not measured on planners.
- Because conversions front-load into week one and monthly retention is poor, a planner that bills monthly is exposed to the cliff every month, while an annual or semester plan sold in the first week banks the year before usage decays. RevenueCat's finding that cheaper apps retain better argues for a low annual price over a high monthly one.
- Habit-forming loops that survive the cliff in this category are external triggers (deadline reminders, LMS sync, teacher-driven use as with ClassDojo/Remind), not the planner's own value; a planner without an external trigger is at the low end of the range.

### Gaps
- No source measures retention specifically for study planners or habit apps among students, and none reports a mid-semester or week-4 churn figure. Adjust's and AppsFlyer's original reports were unreachable; the numbers above are as relayed by secondary sites.
- Sensor Tower's category revenue data is dominated by Duolingo; no planner appears in any top-5 list found.

## Q4. Case studies of small, indie or solo-built student apps that reached meaningful revenue, and post-mortems of student planner startups

### Takeaway
The indie successes found monetise either a one-time iOS purchase that funds a free ecosystem (Anki), a bootstrapped freemium tool compounding for a decade (Doist), or AI study tools grown through student social channels (Turbo AI, Knowt riding Quizlet's paywall). No pure study planner surfaced with public revenue; the largest free planner added ads, and the one well-funded student calendar (Saturn, $53-65M) has no disclosed revenue model. Failure evidence is thin and old but points the same way: building before showing students, and selling something a general tool later does for free.

### Cited Findings
- Anki: a $24.99 one-time iOS app funds the whole free, open-source project including AnkiWeb hosting; the author explicitly cannot take donations because Anki is run as a business — [Anki FAQ](https://faqs.ankiweb.net/how-can-i-donate.html); [Anki support](https://apps.ankiweb.net/support)
- Doist: bootstrapped on ~$40K, $1M ARR only in 2015 (eight years after launch), $14M run-rate by end-2020, $20M in 2023 and $26.5M in 2024 (est.) — [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/doist); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/doist). A 2013 six-year user retrospective exists — [Subtraction.com](https://www.subtraction.com/2013/07/23/six-years-with-todoist/amp) (2013).
- Turbo AI: two 20-year-old dropouts, $750K raised, profitable throughout, 15 staff, 5M users and eight-figure ARR within ~20 months of launch; growth from social-media strategy and staying close to student communities at colleges like UCLA — [Business Insider via AOL](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-olds-grown-ai-note-102101526.html); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users)
- Knowt: $2.58M raised, ~1.8M users by mid-2024, ~$3M revenue (est.), positioned entirely as "the free alternative to Quizlet" after Quizlet paywalled Learn mode — [Crunchbase](https://crunchbase.com/organization/knowt); [Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/c/knowt-revenue); [Blaze](https://blaze.today/blog/knowt-alternatives/); [Learning Counsel](https://thelearningcounsel.com/articles/app-of-the-week/the-1-quizlet-alternative-with-ai-tools-that-make-teaching-easier/)
- Structured: indie German studio, "over 1 million happy planners", Pro sold monthly/annual/lifetime — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/structured-daily-planner/id1499198946)
- Shovel: $9.79/mo or $35/yr with 7-day trial, lifetime SKU on iOS, listed in the Brightspace LMS integration hub — [Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/website/shovel-study-planner); [Brightspace](https://integrationhub.brightspace.com/details/shovel-study-planner). No founder revenue post found.
- MyStudyLife: millions of users, MSL+ at $29.99/yr, and a published rationale for adding ads — [MSL help centre](https://care.mystudylife.com/en/articles/9502668-why-we-re-introducing-ads-on-mystudylife)
- The long tail: a March-2026 daily planner with $17 MRR and 6 subscribers after six months of organic App Store traffic — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/startup/mydailyplanner)
- Saturn: $53M ([Built In NYC](https://www.builtinnyc.com/articles/saturn-raises-44m-hiring-social-calendar-app)) to $65M+ ([F4 Fund](https://app.f4.fund/startups/joinsaturn)) raised for a high-school social calendar; #1 App Store in August 2023 followed by school-district warnings over fake profiles — [Fox 35 Orlando](https://fox35orlando.com/news/local-schools-warn-about-new-saturn-app). No revenue disclosed.
- Chegg and Course Hero as the category's cautionary tale: -40% subscribers in a year at Chegg — [Chegg Q2 2025 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1364954/000136495425000090/a9901-financialresultsq220.htm); first-ever layoffs at Course Hero — [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/16/course-hero-edtech-unicorn-last-valued-at-3-6-billion-conducts-layoffs)
- Post-mortem (old): a student-focused startup that shut down in June 2015 "demonstrated their product to hundreds of students and no one seemed to care about it, and it was the first time they ever demonstrated their product to their end users"; lesson given was validate the problem with the customer segment first — [Tech.co: 30 founders share why their startups failed](https://tech.co/news/50-startups-share-startups-failed-2014-10) (2014-2015). A 2016 Indian college-startup failure essay makes the same point — [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2016/03/college-startup-fail-entrepreneur/) (2016).
- Indie planner launches without student focus: Planrow launched on Indie Hackers after "nearly 2 years of work" — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/launch-ih-planrow-task-management-tool-for-planning-your-day-week-month-and-year-f58ac5a448); the Indie Hackers case-study database is the place to look for more — [Indie Hackers interviews](https://www.indiehackers.com/interview/9a3be77924)

### Inferences
- What worked, by pattern: (1) sell a one-time licence on the platform where students already pay (Anki on iOS) rather than a subscription; (2) let an incumbent's paywall be your marketing (Knowt vs Quizlet); (3) distribute through student social media at near-zero cost with a tiny team (Turbo AI: 15 people, 20K signups/day); (4) get into the institution's LMS so the app has an external trigger (Shovel in Brightspace). None of the successes was won on planner features.
- What killed or capped things: general AI eating the paid unit (Chegg, Course Hero); a free planner with no paid unit beyond "remove ads" (MyStudyLife); social/virality without a revenue model (Saturn); and building for two years before a student saw it (2015 post-mortem, Planrow).
- For a solo founder, the realistic revenue analogue is Anki/iStudiez-scale (a one-time or low annual price on a niche the big tools ignore), not Turbo AI-scale, unless a social-distribution engine exists.

### Gaps
- No founder-written revenue report for any student planner (Shovel, iStudiez, Class Timetable, Power Planner, Egenda) was found before the search budget expired; Indie Hackers and r/SaaS were not searchable directly.
- No post-mortem specifically about a student planner startup was found; the failure evidence above is generic and from 2014-2016.
- Structured's revenue figure could not be tied to a verifiable page (see Q1 gaps).

## Q5. Is "per semester" pricing used anywhere, and how does it perform vs monthly/annual?

### Takeaway
Per-semester pricing exists almost only in institutional courseware and classroom tools (Cengage Unlimited at $119.99/semester, Quest at $25/course/semester, Team+ at $5/student/class/semester, NurseTasks per clinical semester, AbS Plus 5-month billing); none of the consumer study or planner apps found uses it, and no source compares its conversion or retention with monthly or annual plans.

### Cited Findings
- Cengage Unlimited: unlimited access to Cengage digital course materials for $119.99 per semester — [Campus Technology, Dec 2017](https://campustechnology.com/articles/2017/12/05/cengage-announces-subscription-plan.aspx) (2017 launch price; current price not verified)
- Quest Learning & Assessment (UT Austin): $25 per course per semester/quarter — [Quest subscriptions](https://getquest.cns.utexas.edu/subscriptions/)
- Team+ classroom system: $99 per-class subscription or $5 per student, covering one class for one semester — [Team+ pricing](https://teamplus.education/?p=206)
- AbS Plus (Nigeria): semester billing option (5 months) from ₦4,500 for the Basic tier — [AbS pricing](https://www.abstechconnect.com/pricing)
- NurseTasks: option to pay per clinical semester, from $10/month/student — [NurseTasks pricing](https://nursetasks.notion.site/Pricing-a689bd5706434b778560614b52de01fb)
- iClicker publishes student pricing by term — [iClicker pricing](https://iclicker.com/pricing) (amount not captured)
- Consumer study apps found all price monthly/annual (plus lifetime in some): Quizlet $7.99/$35.99 ([ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-quizlet-plus-cost/)); Knowt $5.99/$23.99 ([ToolRadar](https://toolradar.com/tools/knowt/pricing)); Shovel $9.79/$35 ([Educational App Store](https://www.educationalappstore.com/website/shovel-study-planner)); MyStudyLife $4.99/$29.99 ([MSL help](https://care.mystudylife.com/en/articles/9502667-how-to-talk-to-your-parents-about-mystudylife)); iStudiez $2.99/$26.99 ([App Store](https://apple.co/2E5bm2T)); Ahead $9.99/$29.99 ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cd/app/id6756762430)). Chegg's annual plan is $119.40 — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-chegg-cost/) — the same money as one Cengage semester.

### Inferences
- The semester unit is natural where the buyer is a course (courseware, clickers) and unnatural in app stores, which only offer weekly/monthly/2-month/3-month/6-month/annual durations; a "semester pass" in a consumer app is really a 4- or 6-month SKU, which none of the planners found bother to offer.
- Given RevenueCat's data that conversions cluster in the first week and cheaper apps retain better, a low-priced 4-6-month pass sold at term start would match student intent better than monthly billing, but this is untested in any source found.
- The cluster of annual prices at $24-36 (Knowt, MSL, iStudiez, Shovel, Quizlet, Ahead) is the observable market-clearing "year of studying" price; a semester pass would have to land at roughly half that, $12-20.

### Gaps
- No data anywhere on semester-plan conversion, renewal, or churn versus monthly/annual; no consumer study app with a semester SKU was found.
- Cengage Unlimited's and Pearson+'s current (2025-2026) semester/term prices were not verified.

## Q6. Evidence on "AI-powered planner" apps (2024-2026) that charge for AI import/planning, and whether they convert better than plain planners

### Takeaway
A wave of "upload your syllabus" AI planners now exists (Ahead at $9.99/mo or $29.99/yr with a 3-day trial, ProPlan Scholar, PlanMySemester from $4.99/mo, iStudy AI, StudyFetch at $7.99-11.99/mo, and MyStudyLife's own AI schedule scanning inside MSL+), and AI features are priced 2-3x above classic planners; but no public conversion or revenue data exists for any of them, and the only AI study product with disclosed scale (Turbo AI) sells lecture notes, not plans.

### Cited Findings
- Ahead - AI Student Planner: snap a photo or upload a PDF of the syllabus and the AI extracts every assignment, exam, reading and class time into the calendar; $9.99/month or $29.99/year with a 3-day free trial — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/cd/app/id6756762430)
- ProPlan Scholar: upload a syllabus (PDF, Word or photo), AI extracts courses, professors, class times, assignments and exam dates and builds a study schedule; free with in-app purchases — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ca/app/id6777539187)
- PlanMySemester: free AI study tutor, syllabus breakdowns and study guides; paid plans from $4.99/month — [MyStudyLife: Best AI school planner apps 2026](https://mystudylife.com/best-ai-school-planner-apps-for-students-in-2026-free-paid/)
- iStudy AI study assistant / homework helper — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/istudy-ai-study-assistant/id6745830879)
- StudyFetch: upload PDFs, slides and YouTube videos; free basic; $7.99/month entry; $11.99/month Premium — [Tools for Humans](https://toolsforhumans.ai/ai-tools/study-fetch)
- MyStudyLife's paid tier includes "AI schedule scanning" — [MSL help](https://care.mystudylife.com/en/articles/9502667-how-to-talk-to-your-parents-about-mystudylife)
- Quizlet put AI practice tests behind Plus — [ThePricer](https://www.thepricer.org/how-much-does-quizlet-plus-cost/); Knowt lets teachers record a whole class and get study guides, flashcards and a class-specific chatbot — [Learning Counsel](https://thelearningcounsel.com/articles/app-of-the-week/the-1-quizlet-alternative-with-ai-tools-that-make-teaching-easier/)
- Turbo AI: ~$20/month, 5M users, eight-figure ARR for AI note-taking — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/23/20-year-old-dropouts-built-ai-notetaker-turbo-ai-to-5-million-users); [AOL/Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/20-olds-grown-ai-note-102101526.html)
- General AI planners for professionals: Motion $19-34/month — [Ellie](https://ellieplanner.com/productivity-copilot/motion-app-pricing); Amie $25/user/month — [ToolQuestor](https://toolquestor.com/it/vs/amie-vs-motion)
- Platform benchmark relevant to AI-feature paywalls: hard paywalls convert 10.7% vs 2.1% freemium at day 35; 3-day trials lose 84% of cancellers by day 1 — [RevenueCat 2026 via Subscription Insider](https://www.subscriptioninsider.com/blog/revenuecat-data-shows-subscription-app-growth-concentrating-at-the-top-news)

### Inferences
- Syllabus import is a once-per-semester event, which is why Ahead pairs a 3-day trial with an annual price: the trial is long enough to import the term and short enough that the student pays before the week-4 cliff. Monthly billing on a one-shot AI feature invites import-then-cancel.
- AI raises the price ceiling only when it produces recurring output (notes every lecture, practice tests every week). Import-only AI is a conversion hook, not a retention engine; the retention still has to come from the planner's daily loop.
- Akada's Claude MCP connector is the same feature as Ahead's syllabus import, delivered through a tool the student must already use; if it needs a paid Claude plan, the addressable market shrinks to students who already pay for AI, and if it does not, the feature is free for the student and hard to charge for. Either way it is a differentiator against MyStudyLife/iStudiez, not a standalone paid unit, on the evidence here.
- The absence of any AI planner in Sensor Tower's or Appfigures' education top lists, while Turbo AI reached eight-figure ARR, suggests planning-with-AI has not yet produced a breakout; the breakouts are in content generation.

### Gaps
- No conversion, revenue, retention, or review-count data for Ahead, ProPlan Scholar, PlanMySemester, iStudy AI or StudyFetch; App Store pages were unreachable so even rating counts are missing.
- No study compares AI-import planners with plain planners on conversion.
- Whether Claude's MCP connectors are available on the free tier as of Sept 2026 was not checked (no search budget remained); the inference above is conditional on that.
