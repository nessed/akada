## Key question 1: How can a Pakistan-resident solo founder charge in USD internationally (Stripe, MoRs, app stores, payout rails)?

### Takeaway
Stripe still does not onboard Pakistan-based merchants (Pakistan appears in Stripe's own payout docs only as a "cross-border payouts" recipient country), but that same cross-border rail is exactly why three merchant-of-record platforms verifiably pay out to Pakistani bank accounts today: Polar (via Stripe Connect Express, 5% + 50c), Creem (via Wise-partner bank transfer, 3.9% + 40c, individual accounts likely only), and Gumroad (PKR bank payouts, 10% + 50c + processing). Paddle also lists Pakistan as not-excluded (reported payouts by bank/PayPal/Payoneer), while Dodo Payments and Lemon Squeezy are unverified-to-contradictory for Pakistan, and PayPal remains absent.

### Inferences
- The practical ranking for an individual in Lahore today: Polar (verified list, lowest friction because KYC is Stripe Express on a personal CNIC + PKR bank account) > Creem (verified list, but "individual accounts only" restriction likely, $7-or-1% payout fee makes small payouts expensive) > Paddle (not excluded, most mature, heavier onboarding, Payoneer/bank payout) > Gumroad (verified PKR payouts but 10% + 2.9% + 50c is punitive for a low-priced subscription) > Lemon Squeezy/Dodo (unverified or reportedly unavailable). None of these require a registered company for an individual; all will want CNIC, address proof and a Pakistani bank account in the founder's own name.
- Because every MoR's payout ultimately arrives as an inward foreign remittance to a Pakistani bank, the founder's SBP/PSEB/FBR position (Key question 3) is the same regardless of which MoR is chosen; the MoR does not change tax residency or PSEB eligibility.
- Fee stack for a $5/month plan sold through Polar Starter to a non-US card: 5% + 50c + 1.5% = about $0.83 (16.5%), before the payout-side Stripe fees and the bank's inward-remittance FX spread; at $3/month the fixed 50c alone is 17%. Low price points push towards annual or termly billing to amortise fixed fees.
- Selling to Pakistani students in USD through a foreign MoR is possible in principle (Dodo/Polar accept Pakistani cards) but is structurally bad for the domestic market: buyers pay an international-transaction FX markup, plus the s.236Y advance tax on international card spend (see Key question 3), and few students hold internationally-enabled cards (Key question 2). A local PKR rail for Pakistan and an MoR for everyone else is the pattern several Pakistani developers describe ("JazzCash/Easypaisa via a local PSP can be a second provider behind the same interface" — [make-a-calculater SPEC.md](https://github.com/faaz0saleem/make-a-calculater/blob/main/SPEC.md)).

### Gaps
- Stripe's current official supported-country list and any "preview/invite-only" status for Pakistan could not be fetched (stripe.com blocked); the mirror text ("only available for Cross-border payouts accounts") is the strongest available evidence and matches every third-party report found.
- Lemon Squeezy's official country list (and whether Stripe's ownership since 2024 has changed Pakistan eligibility, or whether LS is still onboarding new merchants) could not be verified; only two Pakistani developers' claims that LS lists Pakistan for USD bank payouts, versus one Aug-2026 note that LS relies on PayPal for non-bank countries.
- Dodo Payments' official merchant-country list is unverified; its marketing blog implies Pakistan is fine while an Aug-2026 independent check says Pakistan is not listed. Needs a direct check of docs.dodopayments.com or a support ticket.
- Paddle's payout methods for Pakistan (bank wire vs Payoneer), minimum payout and KYC documents were not verifiable from a primary page.
- FastSpring eligibility for Pakistan: no source found.
- Google Play's current (2026) merchant-country page is on support.google.com, which was blocked; the only evidence is a 2014 announcement. Apple's list of countries eligible for the Paid Apps agreement/bank payouts was not found on a fetchable page (the help URL returned 404).
- Stripe Atlas eligibility for Pakistan residents and the current stance of US neobanks (Mercury etc.) toward Pakistan-resident founders: not researched due to budget; several sources flag "US LLC via Stripe Atlas" as the route that "opens every provider", but with tax consequences.

## Key question 2: Local rails in PKR (Safepay, PayFast, JazzCash, Easypaisa, XPay, Bank Alfalah, Foree, SadaPay/NayaPay) and cards vs wallets among students

### Takeaway
Safepay is the only local gateway with documented subscription/recurring support (via Visa Cybersource, SBP-approved 2022) and published pricing (2.9% + Rs30 domestic cards, 3.2% + Rs30 international, 2.5% bank, ex-tax); Easypaisa lets an individual/sole proprietor open a merchant account with a Rs 500,000 monthly limit; direct JazzCash/Easypaisa gateway agreements are separate, document-heavy processes. Card penetration is low and laptop ownership is 14% of households, so wallet-based (JazzCash/Easypaisa/Raast) collection is a necessity rather than an option for a mass student market, and true card-on-file recurring billing is the exception in Pakistan.

### Cited Findings
- Safepay is a Karachi fintech, Y Combinator-backed — [KrASIA](https://kr-asia.com/pakistani-fintech-firm-safepay-receives-y-combinators-backing); it received SBP pilot approval and "went live with Visa's Cybersource" in Aug 2022, enabling "functionality like subscription payments" — [ProPakistani, 17 Aug 2022 (snippet)](https://propakistani.pk/2022/08/17/karachi-based-fintech-safepay-gets-sbp-approval-goes-live-with-visas-cybersource/); an integrator lists Safepay as offering "full API support, subscription/recurring billing support, multi-currency, and Visa/Mastercard acceptance" — [BillingServ integration page](https://cdn.billingserv.com/integrations/safepay/)
- Safepay pricing: "2.9% + Rs30 (Exclusive of tax) for domestic transactions and 3.2% + Rs30 (Exclusive of tax) for international transactions"; bank-based payments "2.5%" per successful transaction — [Safepay help: How much does Safepay charge (snippet)](https://safepay.helpscoutdocs.com/article/120-how-much-does-safepay-charge); [Safepay fee breakdown (snippet)](https://safepay.helpscoutdocs.com/article/131-safepay-fee-breakdown); an older report cited "3.3 per cent commission plus Rs30" — [Dawn (snippet)](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1542800)

- Pakistani developers' own working assumption: "For local students, JazzCash/Easypaisa via a local PSP can be a second provider" — [make-a-calculater SPEC.md](https://github.com/faaz0saleem/make-a-calculater/blob/main/SPEC.md)

### Inferences
- For an individual student founder, Easypaisa's merchant account (individual/sole-proprietor eligible, Rs 500k/month cap) is the lowest-friction way to accept PKR today; Safepay is the only local option that looks like "Stripe Billing" (card tokenisation + subscriptions) but expects KYC that, for a sole proprietor, in practice means CNIC + NTN + bank account (see Key question 3), and its Rs30 fixed fee is 10% of a Rs300 charge.
- Given 14% household computer ownership nationally, Akada's web-first product implicitly targets the laptop-owning minority — which LUMS/IBA/NUST students largely are — but a mass-market Pakistani plan would need wallet-based, manually renewed (not auto-recurring) payments, as wallet rails do not offer card-on-file recurring billing.
- Realistic PKR fee stack via Safepay on a Rs 500 monthly charge: Rs 14.5 + Rs 30 = Rs 44.5 (8.9%) plus provincial sales tax on the fee, before the 1% s.6A withholding by the payment intermediary (Key question 3).

### Gaps
- No official fee schedules were retrievable for PayFast, XPay, JazzCash, Easypaisa online gateway, Bank Alfalah's gateway, or Foree; whether any of them support card-on-file recurring for a sole proprietor is unknown (PayFast "basic recurring" is the only secondary claim).
- Bank Alfalah payment gateway, Foree, SadaPay (business accounts) and NayaPay (business/freelancer accounts) returned nothing usable within the budget; their 2026 status is unknown.
- No SBP Payment Systems Review statistics (number of debit/credit cards, e-commerce card share vs cash-on-delivery, Raast P2M volumes) were obtained; the search budget ran out before this query.
- No data on card ownership specifically among university students, nor on student-segment usage of JazzCash/Easypaisa.

## Key question 3: Regulation and tax for an individual founder (SBP export proceeds, PSEB, FBR, domestic e-commerce tax, buyer-side taxes)

### Takeaway
An individual can do all of this without a company: get an NTN via FBR IRIS, register with PSEB as a freelancer (Rs 1,000; Rs 2,000 annual renewal) to have foreign SaaS receipts taxed at a final 0.25% (1% if unregistered) under s.154A, keep up to 50% or USD 5,000/month of proceeds in an Exporters' Special Foreign Currency Account, and file annually; the 0.25% regime was extended to 30 June 2029 in the June-2026 budget. Domestic PKR sales are a different regime: since Finance Act 2025, payment intermediaries withhold a final 1% of gross on digitally-ordered goods/services (s.6A), online sellers face sales-tax registration obligations (s.14(1A)/(1B)), software is a provincially-taxed service (PRA in Punjab), and Pakistani buyers paying foreign vendors by card bear s.236Y advance tax (5%/10% historically; a 2026 dataset shows 0.5%/1% — conflict flagged).

### Cited Findings

Foreign (export) receipts:
- "Federal Budget 2026-27 presented June 12, 2026, Finance Minister Muhammad Aurangzeb confirmed that the concessional 0.25% Final Tax Regime (FTR) for IT export income is extended through June 30, 2029"; rates: "1% Tax Rate: The default for IT export proceeds received through approved banking channels (Section 154A withholding) if you are not PSEB-registered... 0.25% Tax Rate: The concessional rate for PSEB-registered IT exporters and freelancers on foreign remittances received through banking channels, provided the 80% remittance rule and other conditions are met"; "the bank withholds at receipt; the withheld amount is generally final" — [beancount.io guide, 13 Aug 2026 (snippet)](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/13/pakistan-it-freelancers-0-25-percent-pseb-2029-guide); Section 65F / s.154A framing also in [CSSPrep guide (snippet)](https://cssprep.com.pk/freelancer-income-tax-pakistan/); [ETTC 2026 guide](https://ettc.pk/blog/freelancer-tax-in-pakistan-2026)
- PSEB registration: "The new registration fee is PKR 1,000, with annual renewal at PKR 2,000"; a freelancer "must hold a valid CNIC and an active individual NTN from FBR", plus "bank account details, proof of freelance work (contracts, invoices), and evidence of past remittances"; certificate "typically within two to three weeks" — [paktaxcalculator.pk guide (snippet)](https://paktaxcalculator.pk/pseb-freelancer-registration); [Global Law Experts (snippet)](https://globallawexperts.com/how-to-get-registered-with-pseb-in-pakistan/); NTN is obtained on FBR's IRIS portal — [startup.pk (snippet)](https://startup.pk/ntn-registration-pakistan/)
- PSEB "is a statutory body operating under the Ministry of Information Technology and Telecommunication... for IT and IT-enabled services companies, freelancers and call centres that export" — [nsave guide (snippet)](https://www.nsave.com/article/the-smart-freelancers-guide-to-taxes-in-pakistan-2025)
- SBP: "increased the permissible retention limit of IT exporters from 35% to 50% of their export proceeds in the Exporters' Specialized Foreign Currency Accounts (ESFCAs)" — [APP (snippet)](https://www.app.com.pk/?p=701831); freelancer framework: accounts can be opened "digitally and physically... with minimum documentation", ESFCA "opened concurrently with... their primary PKR account", freelancers "could retain 50% of their export proceeds or USD 5,000 per month—whichever is higher—in their ESFCAs and could make all payments from those accounts without any approval", and banks were told to issue debit cards on ESFCA balances — [The News (snippet)](https://www.thenews.pk/print/1408546-sbp-launches-new-measures-to-boost-it-exports); [Profit, 10 Jan 2026 (snippet)](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/?p=156993); a further SBP EPD Circular Letter No. 06 dated 2026-04-06 exists — [regalert.today](https://regalert.today/document/c46c1fb6-df00-4378-8a81-e6dd3190efcc/text); [SBP FECL6 annexure PDF (not fetchable)](https://www.sbp.org.pk/epd/2026/FECL6-Annexure.pdf); banks market dedicated "IT Exporter & Freelancer" accounts — [BankIslami](https://bankislami.com.pk/ur/?p=77626)
- Digital-Presence Proceeds Tax (5% on foreign digital vendors, enacted June 2025) "was suspended by SRO 1366(I)/2025 effective 1 July 2025"; the SRO is at [download1.fbr.gov.pk (not fetched)](https://download1.fbr.gov.pk/SROs/20257302072529390SRO1366(I)2025.pdf) — [barriovibe research notes](https://github.com/usmankhan045/barriovibe/blob/main/research/OPEN-ITEMS.md); the Act's enactment and industry pushback were reported by Tribune — [quoted in a 20 Jul 2025 page](https://github.com/zeeshanali90233/Ideoversity-Web-with-Agentic-Ai-B1/blob/main/html/intro(20-7-25)/index.html)

Domestic (PKR) sales:
- "Section 6A taxes payments for digitally ordered goods and services delivered from within Pakistan: 1% collected by the payment intermediary where you are paid digitally, and 2% collected by the courier on cash on delivery. It is charged on gross receipts, not profit"; it is "final by default below Rs 200 million of turnover, and adjustable above it under section 6A(3)"; "The proviso to section 6A(1) excludes export proceeds subjected to withholding under sections 154 or 154A, so a PSEB-registered exporter withheld at 0.25% is outside the section" — [barriovibe sales-tax guide, published 13 Sep 2026 (secondary; quotes statute)](https://github.com/usmankhan045/barriovibe/blob/main/content/guides/salestax.ts)
- "Sections 14(1A) and 14(1B), inserted by the Finance Act 2025... Any person, including a non-resident, selling digitally ordered goods from within Pakistan through an online marketplace, website or software application must apply for registration... an online marketplace or courier may not allow anyone to use its services for e-commerce unless that person holds an NTN and, where 14(1A) applies, sales tax registration"; after FBR sales-tax registration "you must visit a NADRA e-Sahulat centre within thirty days for biometric verification" — [same guide](https://github.com/usmankhan045/barriovibe/blob/main/content/guides/salestax.ts)
- "Goods are federal and go to FBR. Services are provincial: Punjab to the PRA, Sindh to the SRB... A software house or agency supplies services, so its obligation is provincial"; "There is no turnover threshold in [section 14]" — [same guide](https://github.com/usmankhan045/barriovibe/blob/main/content/guides/salestax.ts)

Buyer-side tax when a Pakistani pays a foreign vendor by card (relevant if Akada bills Pakistanis through a foreign MoR):
- "Under Section 236Y, filers pay 5% and non-filers pay 10% advance tax on international payments made through Pakistani credit, debit, or prepaid cards"; it "applies to online shopping... Netflix, Amazon, university fees, and all foreign transactions. Tax is adjustable in annual return" — [TaxBuddy FAQ text](https://github.com/Umair-Mubeen/TaxBuddyUmair/blob/main/TaxBuddy/TaxBuddyApp/views.py); worked example "Bank withheld Rs. 40,000 (5%)" on Rs 800,000 of card spend — [pakistani-tax-advisor help text](https://github.com/MasoodZaf/pakistani-tax-advisor/blob/main/Frontend/src/help/adjustableTaxHelp.js)
- Conflicting: the same TaxBuddy project's 2026-27 rate table records "Section 236Y | Foreign card payments (debit/credit/prepaid) | 0.5% | 1%" (filer / non-filer) — [seed_wht_2026.py](https://github.com/Umair-Mubeen/TaxBuddyUmair/blob/main/TaxBuddy/TaxBuddyApp/management/commands/seed_wht_2026.py). This suggests the rate was cut for tax year 2026-27 but is unverified against FBR.

### Inferences
- Sequence for a student founder before the first invoice: (1) NTN on IRIS as an individual; (2) open a bank account and ask for the freelancer ESFCA pairing; (3) PSEB freelancer registration (needs some remittance history, so the first few MoR payouts may land at 1% before the 0.25% rate applies); (4) file the annual return even at zero tax. No SECP company is needed for any of this; a company only becomes relevant if a foreign entity (US LLC) is chosen to unlock Stripe, which changes tax residency questions.
- For domestic PKR sales the founder should budget: gateway fee (about 3% + Rs30) + 1% s.6A withholding (final) + provincial sales tax on the software service (Punjab PRA rate for IT services not verified here) and should expect the gateway to demand NTN and possibly sales-tax registration at onboarding because s.14(1B) makes intermediaries the enforcers.
- Billing Pakistani students in USD through a foreign MoR would layer s.236Y advance tax (0.5-10% depending on year and filer status) and bank FX markups on top of the MoR fees, on buyers who mostly are not tax filers; this is a second strong reason for a separate PKR rail.

### Gaps
- Punjab Revenue Authority's sales-tax rate on IT/software services for an individual seller (and whether a small sole proprietor must register) was not verified.
- Whether s.14(1A) sales-tax registration covers digitally-ordered *services* (the guide's wording says "goods") is unclear; the guide is a secondary source (it quotes statute text but is a private site's content file).
- The current s.236Y rate (5%/10% vs 0.5%/1%) needs a check against the Finance Act 2026 / FBR withholding card.
- The content of SBP EPD Circular Letter No. 06 (April 2026) is unknown; it may have changed freelancer account rules again.
- Whether a MoR payout (e.g. Polar's Stripe transfer) is coded by Pakistani banks as an IT-export remittance eligible for s.154A treatment, versus a general inward remittance, was not verifiable; PSEB guides speak of "foreign remittances received through banking channels" and an "80% remittance rule".
- Roshan Digital Account is for non-resident Pakistanis and did not surface as relevant to a resident founder; not researched further.

## Key question 7: How Pakistan-based founders actually bill global customers, and the obstacles they report

### Takeaway
Reported practice from Lahore/Karachi founders and developers converges on: no PayPal, no direct Stripe, Payoneer as the default inflow (with fee increases in 2025), a merchant-of-record (Paddle, Polar, Lemon Squeezy) or a foreign entity for card billing, and local wallets for Pakistani buyers. Named, sourceable success stories of *indie SaaS* billing globally from Pakistan are thin; the best-documented cases are freelancers and fintechs built to solve the payments gap itself (Safepay, PaySay).

### Cited Findings
- "PayPal is not available in Pakistan, and Stripe does not support Pakistani entities. This creates a significant barrier for Pakistani developers trying to build global businesses"; the MoR route lets you "sell software globally, accept payments in USD, and receive payouts directly in Pakistan without ever needing a PayPal or Stripe account" — [Dodo blog (snippet)](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/sell-software-from-pakistan)
- A Pakistani freelancer profiled by Payoneer processes "90-95% of payments... through Payoneer" — [Payoneer success story](https://www.payoneer.com/success-stories/syed-arsalan-ali-shah/); Payoneer's 2025 fee changes "sparked frustration among Pakistani freelancers who depend on the platform for cross-border payments" — [Profit (snippet)](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2025/05/03/payoneer-slaps-3-withdrawal-fee-on-pakistani-users-sparking-backlash-among-freelancers)
- PaySay, "a fintech headquartered in UK with an offshore office in Lahore, aims to enable local freelancers and education academies receive payments from Europe by offering them a UK-based bank account" — [Dawn (snippet)](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1489894); Safepay's co-founder Raza Naqvi is "a Karachi-born solicitor practicing in London, who left his job in London to pursue Safepay" — [Dawn (snippet)](https://www.dawn.com/news/amp/1542800); Profit asked "Can Safepay become the Pakistani equivalent of Stripe" — [Profit, Mar 2021](https://profit.pakistantoday.com.pk/2021/03/07/can-safepay-become-the-pakistani-equivalent-of-stripe)
- Developer notes from Pakistani repos: "Pakistan cannot open Stripe on a local CNIC/bank — use Lemon Squeezy, Paddle, Payoneer, or a legal company in a Stripe country with a bank there" — [web-life payouts.tsx](https://github.com/burhan229uddin-sys/web-life/blob/main/payouts.tsx); "Stripe does not support businesses domiciled in Pakistan, and this is the single thing most likely to block your launch. Do not hard-code a provider. Build a PaymentProvider interface" and "Paddle or Lemon Squeezy (merchant-of-record... can pay you out to a PK bank via wire/Payoneer), 2Checkout/Verifone, or a US/UAE entity + Stripe. For local students, JazzCash/Easypaisa via a local PSP" — [make-a-calculater SPEC.md](https://github.com/faaz0saleem/make-a-calculater/blob/main/SPEC.md); an app-strategy note recommends "web checkout via Lemon Squeezy/Paddle while you also distribute the APK directly... then add Play Billing if/when you go big" and to "verify Pakistan payout support before relying on it" — [professor-ielts strategy note](https://github.com/mdmedica786-code/professor-ielts/blob/main/STRATEGY-AND-COMPETITORS.md)
- A comparable South-Asian founder (Bangladesh, Aug 2026) chose Polar because Stripe "was never actually available", found Dodo did not list his country, and kept Paddle as fallback; he notes MoR terms flow into the product's ToS/privacy policy ("our Privacy Policy lists Polar as a sub-processor") — [postbag ADR-007](https://github.com/faahim/postbag/blob/main/docs/decisions/ADR-007-billing-provider.md)
- Dodo's case study of an Indian indie founder rejected by Stripe illustrates the same rejection pattern — [Dodo case study: IndieKit](https://dodopayments.com/case-studies/indiekit); [Dodo: why indie hackers lose revenue without a MoR](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/indie-hackers-lose-revenue-without-merchant-of-record)
- Shopify merchants in Pakistan repeatedly ask which provider works and report Payoneer/2Checkout frictions — [Shopify Community: Shopify payment in Pakistan](https://community.shopify.com/t/shopify-payment-in-pakistan/212936); [Can we use Payoneer for payments on Shopify in Pakistan?](https://community.shopify.com/t/can-we-use-payoneer-for-payments-on-shopify-in-pakistan/138552)
- A bootstrapped founder "starting from a small town in Pakistan" (Sohail Sajid) reached "over 50,000 customers in over 150 countries" — but he built the companies from Amsterdam (Imfiy B.V.), i.e. through a Dutch entity — [IPS News PR, 22 Jun 2026 (press release, low authority)](https://ipsnews.net/business/2026/06/22/sohail-sajid-marks-milestone-with-over-50000-customers-through-bootstrapped-innovation/)

### Inferences
- The pattern across every source is "MoR for the world, wallet for Pakistan, Payoneer/ESFCA for inflows"; the founders who scaled global SaaS from Pakistan generally did it through a foreign entity (UK/NL/US), which is the "company decision with tax consequences" a student should defer until revenue justifies it.
- Chargebacks are handled by the MoR (Polar: $15/dispute; Dodo: $30/dispute); the founder's real exposure is holds and payout minimums ($50 Creem, $100 Gumroad, Creem's $7-or-1% payout fee), which matter at the few-hundred-dollars-a-month scale a student planner would start at.

### Gaps
- No first-person X/LinkedIn/Reddit posts by Lahore/Karachi SaaS founders about payment holds or MoR KYC could be retrieved (those domains are blocked and the search budget ran out); the evidence is vendor blogs, developer repo notes and news coverage.
- No verified example of a Pakistan-resident individual (no foreign entity) running a subscription SaaS at meaningful scale through Polar/Paddle/Creem; the primary sources prove eligibility, not outcomes.
- PayPal's 2026 status (any partnership-based entry into Pakistan announced by the Ministry of IT) was not checked.
